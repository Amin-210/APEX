## 1. NorthPower (North Alpine) - men's compression shaping tank - US / English - analyzed 09/2026

Product type: PROBLEM / LOSS.
Success signals: large review count, many ad variations, bundle offer scaling (not verified with sales data).

| Layer | What they knew |
|---|---|
| Who | Men roughly 27-55, regular bodies, not gym people |
| Problem in his words | "Dad bod", "beer belly", "man boobs", "love handles" |
| Hidden habits | Hides under baggy shirts, wears two layers, avoids mirrors and photos, buys suits too big |
| Painful moments | Getting dressed, summer, the beach, family photos, events, the office camera |
| Surface desire | Look slimmer, flatter belly, clothes that fit |
| Deep desire | Feel confident, in control of his body again, proud, seen |
| Identity | A strong man again, not someone "wearing shapewear" |
| Fears / objections | Does it actually work? Will people notice? Is it hot? Will it fit? Am I too old? Is it a scam? |
| Failed solutions | Diets and gym take time, baggy clothes only hide |
| Who he wants to impress | Girlfriend, wife, son, colleagues, himself in the mirror |

3 levels: functional = flatten belly, hold in chest / emotional = confident, not ashamed, in control / identity = a strong man his family is proud of.

Key insight: built around the man's lost confidence, not around a compression shirt.
Awareness: problem-aware (knows his belly, not the product). Sophistication: high (compression shirts known) → needs a "new mechanism" angle.
Don't copy: pseudo-science mechanism, weight-loss overclaims, inconsistent review counts and facts, brand-written testimonials.

## 2. YURI - collagen overnight wrapping (peel-off) mask - cosmetics - US / English - analyzed 09/2026

Product type: PROBLEM / LOSS.
Success signals: 1000+ active ads, Amazon knock-offs using its name ("Official YURI" branding to fight them), price tests, 3-tier bundle + subscription, SEO blog push. Review numbers inconsistent. Rides a category made viral on TikTok by Medicube (demand partly pre-existing).

| Layer | What they knew |
|---|---|
| Who | Women ~25-50, US, TikTok skincare / K-beauty followers, busy or tired, first signs of aging |
| Problem in her words | "Dull", "tired skin", "big pores", "dry flaky patches", "fine lines", "the bounce is gone" |
| Hidden habits | Covers skin with heavy foundation, checks pores close in the mirror, quits long routines |
| Painful moments | Morning mirror, makeup sitting badly on dry patches, winter, looking exhausted after bad sleep / night shifts, turning 40+ |
| Surface desire | Glowing, smooth, hydrated skin, small pores - "glass skin" |
| Deep desire | Look rested and younger, pretty without makeup, zero effort |
| Identity | Woman with naturally good skin and a calm self-care ritual; K-beauty insider, not "anti-wrinkle cream" buyer |
| Fears / objections | Does it work? Breakouts? Is the film normal? Sticky pillow? Fits my routine? How fast? What if it fails? |
| Failed solutions | Sheet masks, night creams, 10-step routines, makeup that only covers |
| Who she wants to impress | Herself in the mirror first; then daughter, compliments, going out makeup-free |

3 levels: functional = hydrate, firm, smaller pores / emotional = rested, pampered, pretty without makeup / identity = "naturally great skin", ages gracefully, effortless ritual.

Key insight: she doesn't want another skincare step - she wants to wake up looking rested and a bit younger while sleep does the work.
Awareness: solution- to product-aware (category already viral) → first screen jumps straight to product + "#1" + "3X collagen". Sophistication: high (stage 4) → bigger claim, authority, "Official", real mechanism (occlusion).
Public reason: glow / glass skin (headline, icons). Secret reason: aging and looking tired (image badge, one icon, age-45 review).
Structure note: heavy LOGIC middle (stats → ingredients → ritual steps → mechanism timeline → comparison table) for a skeptical, ingredient-literate buyer.
Don't copy: fake event timer, rolling "order by today" date, pre-checked subscription, shifting compare-at prices, inconsistent review counts, "self-test clinical" stats, "3X" without comparison, questionable origin claim, too-perfect reviews.

## 3. Aurivo ReHears - red-light "hearing therapy" neckband - senior health device - US / English (German-run) - analyzed 09/2026

Product type: PROBLEM / LOSS.
Success signals: 1000+ active ads, partner bundle + cart upsells.

| Layer | What they knew |
|---|---|
| Who | Seniors 60-80 with hearing loss / tinnitus; also spouse or adult child buying for them |
| Problem in his words | "Sorry, what?", "voices sound muffled", "louder but not clearer", ringing |
| Hidden habits | Nods along, guesses words, stays home, turns TV up, avoids noisy restaurants |
| Painful moments | Family dinners, a daughter's birthday, group talk, restaurants, grandkids, no sleep from ringing |
| Surface desire | Understand speech clearly, stop the ringing |
| Deep desire | Be part of the conversation again, not "watching from the sidelines" |
| Identity | Still an active, capable person - not "an old man with a hearing aid" |
| Fears / objections | Does it work? Is red light safe? Scam? Can I drop hearing aids? Too late for me? Complicated? |
| Failed solutions | Hearing aids: $4,600, louder not clearer, upkeep, discomfort, stigma |
| Who he wants to impress | Grandchildren, spouse, sister, his doctor, himself |

3 levels: functional = understand speech, less ringing, no expensive aid / emotional = not tired, embarrassed or left out / identity = connected family member, not "the old one".

Key insight: he isn't afraid of bad ears - he is afraid of slowly disappearing from his family's conversations.
Awareness: solution-aware (knows and dislikes hearing aids) → first screen = guarantee + new mechanism. Sophistication: stage 3-4.
Public reason: hear better. Secret reason: avoid hearing-aid stigma and looking old (+ cost) - carried by visuals (headphone look, active-senior collage), never said.
Secret stigma of the known solution: the hearing aid itself.
Structure note: very long logic middle (problem explainer → price bar → "for all types" list → 7/30/90 tabs → mechanism → stats → expert → comparison table); no buy button after the emotional peak.
Don't copy: hair-cell regeneration and "restored hearing" claims, "drop your hearing aids" / cochlear-implant advice, fake media quotes, invented institute badge, stock doctor, fake "today only", inconsistent numbers, unusable guarantee.

## 4. Lumi Beam - smart mini projector (HY300-style white label) - consumer electronics / lifestyle - US, UK, EU, AU / English (likely UK-run) - analyzed 09/2026

Product type: DESIRE / LIFESTYLE (nothing was lost - the buyer wants a new pleasure).
Success signals: store running ~10 months with images still updated (Aug 2026), several landing pages for one product (-1, -lp3), clone domain copying it, social accounts on 5 platforms, Instagram ads confirmed by buyers, completion bundle + cart upsells.

| Layer | What they knew |
|---|---|
| Who | Three sub-audiences on one page: young adults 18-40 in small rooms (renters, students, gamers); cozy / BookTok readers (mostly women) wanting "ambient scenes"; couples and parents for movie nights; gift buyers |
| Problem in their words | "No space for a big TV", "watching Netflix on my phone in bed", "bare boring wall", "the TV is taken" (partner watching football) |
| Hidden habits | Watches films on a phone/laptop in bed; plays YouTube "fake window / fireplace / rain" videos for atmosphere; fights over the TV |
| Painful moments | Boring weekend evenings, rainy days, tiny screen in bed, harsh room light while winding down |
| Surface desire | Giant screen anywhere, cheap, no setup, apps built in |
| Deep desire | Escape - a room that turns into a cinema, fantasy library or cabin when the lights go off; a small daily treat |
| Identity | Someone with a cool, cozy, aesthetic room; a "book lover" with a magical nook; the fun partner/parent - not someone who bought "a cheap gadget" |
| Fears / objections | Dim or blurry? Loud fan? Hard to set up / crooked picture? A cheap fake? Works on my ceiling? In daylight? Sound enough? Need a laptop? |
| Failed solutions | Phone/laptop screen; cheap projectors (dim, laggy, loud); expensive projectors ($400+); ambient lamps ($200+); a second TV |
| Who they want to impress | Partner, kids, friends at movie night, their own feed (room aesthetic), themselves ("my weekend") - weak on the page, carried by videos |

3 levels: functional = big, straight, clear picture anywhere, no TV to buy / emotional = cozy escape, excitement, calm wind-down / identity-social = "my room is special", "I'm a cozy / book / movie person", the one who hosts the fun night.

Key insight: they don't want a projector - they want an ordinary small room (especially the bed) to turn into an escape the moment the lights go off.
Awareness: solution- to product-aware (this projector shape is viral and everywhere). Sophistication: stage 5 → no hook on the first screen (name + descriptive line + use-case list + discount); competes by IDENTIFICATION ("the Original" vs fakes) and by REPOSITIONING into a new use + tribe (reading ambiance, scene packs) instead of a new mechanism.
Public reason: movie nights, gaming, streaming (headline, bullets, top half). Secret reason: an aesthetic cozy room / living inside the book's world; not paying for or making space for a TV - carried by videos, free scene packs, reading testimonials and FAQ.
Structure note: logic-heavy (~70%) - feature blocks each answer one fear, emotion comes from dark-room bed/ceiling videos; completion bundle with "choose your free packs"; anti-knockoff slider; price bar vs categories; 20-question FAQ with one honest "no".
Don't copy: "patented / original" claims on a white-label product, daylight / any-surface brightness claims, "replaces your Smart TV", "Lifetime Guarantee" next to 30-day returns (and weak refunds in practice), fake timers and "1% OFF" anchors, wrong-currency price image, generic brand-written testimonials, undelivered gift packs, unlicensed book-world scenes.

## 5. le D'affaires - 5-in-1 rotary cheese grater (white-label kitchen prep tool) - US / English - analyzed 09/2026

Product type: MIX - problem side = chore relief (slow, slipping, knuckle-scraping grating); desire side = "cooking is fun again", "I feel like a pro", family kitchen moment. The page sells mostly from the problem side; the desire side lives in the hero image and reviews.
Success signals: 1000+ ads, Amazon/Walmart sellers put the brand name in their own grater titles (people search the brand), TikTok discovery pages for the brand name, separate "try" ad domain, bundles with a gift.

| Layer | What they knew |
|---|---|
| Who | US home cooks, mostly women ~30-60 cooking for a family; men who like to cook; older people whose hands tire |
| Problem in their words | Grating is slow, messy, awkward; the grater slips; too many tools / clutter (weak on the page - almost no slang) |
| Hidden habits | Holding the grater with one hand and the food with the other; owning many single tools; [HYPOTHESIS] buying pre-shredded cheese, skipping recipes that need julienne |
| Painful moments | Busy weeknight dinners, big prep jobs, scraping knuckles at the end of a carrot, grater sliding on the counter |
| Surface desire | Faster prep, even cuts, one tool for everything, stable base |
| Deep desire | Cooking feels easy and fun again - enjoy the kitchen instead of dreading it |
| Identity | A skilled, confident cook with a "gourmet" kitchen - not someone struggling with a cheap box grater |
| Fears / objections | Will I cut myself? Will it slip? Cheap plastic? Complicated to assemble? Hard veggies? Dishwasher safe? Will it arrive? |
| Failed solutions | Box grater (slow, slips, scrapes knuckles); knives and mandolines (dangerous); many single-purpose gadgets |
| Who they want to impress | Kids and family at the table; themselves as a "good cook" |

3 levels: functional = grate, slice, julienne fast, safely and evenly / emotional = relaxed and in control, cooking is fun, not a chore / identity-social = "a capable home cook with a nice kitchen", the mom whose kids help and enjoy the food.

Key insight: they don't want a grater - they want the annoying, knuckle-scraping part of cooking to disappear so cooking feels like a fun family moment again.
Awareness: product-aware (rotary graters viral, knockoffs everywhere) → no emotional headline; first screen = award badge + name + social-number benefit checklist. Sophistication: stage 5 → competes with a premium brand identity (French-sounding name, "Gourmet", "European chefs"), odd-precision numbers (12.3x, 13.2s, 9 mins) and named parts ("Food Propeller", "Rocker").
Public reason: save time, stay safe (checklist, stats, comparison table). Secret reason: feel like a skilled cook and create a family kitchen moment (hero image, reviews, pro-chef wording, sage-green color).
Structure note: fear-removal machine (layer 8 strongest): guarantee as the FIRST checklist bullet, safety bullet, feature trio, stopwatch demo video, stat strip, attachment carousel, named-parts hotspot, "Install in 6 Easy Steps", Us vs Others vs box grater, troubleshooting FAQ. Emotion only from images, UGC videos and reviews; no verdict grid, no long emotional mechanism.
GAP / opportunity: pre-shredded cheese is the real failed solution for cheese (people pay more for bags of cheese that often contain anti-caking additives) - the page never mentions it. Also no hidden habit or painful moment in any headline.
Don't copy: invented award, fake "as seen in" media quotes, unnamed "European celebrity chef" / celebrity name in the guarantee, unmeasured stats, "0% risk of injury" / "100% safe", "100% dishwasher safe" vs "no hot water" in the FAQ, contradicting guarantees (30 vs 15 days, 2-year vs 30-day), "Fast U.S. Shipping" vs 10-15 days, fake 50% flash sale, repeated reviewer names with different quotes.
