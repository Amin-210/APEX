# Phase 2 — The customer mind map (the source of every line)

No line of copy is written from the supplier text. The supplier gives facts; the map gives meaning, and the framework slots are filled from the map.

## What to produce

**Step 0 — product type and sophistication.** PROBLEM (something is wrong in her life) · DESIRE (nothing is wrong, she wants a feeling) · MIX (a chore: relief plus a little joy). Then the market's sophistication stage, which decides whether the page can win on a promise (early stage) or must win on mechanism, honest comparison and trust (late stage). Most COD gadget markets are late stage (the example market, Kenya, usually is).

**The 10 layers**, each in her words, each tagged [VOC] / [RESEARCH] / [HYPOTHESIS]:
1. Who she is — age, work, estate or town, household
2. The problem in her words — her exact phrases, in every language and slang she mixes (example market: English, Swahili, Sheng)
3. Hidden habits — what she does to hide, avoid or cope, the slightly embarrassing ones
4. Painful or wanting moments — the specific hour, day and place it bites
5. Surface desire — what she says she wants
6. Deep desire — what she actually wants to feel
7. Identity — who she becomes, and who she refuses to be
8. Fears and objections — ranked 1-5 by strength
9. Failed solutions — what she already tried, what it cost, why it failed
10. Who she wants to impress — the person whose reaction matters

**Portrait.** One named person, a day with the problem, the mirror moment, her inner monologue in her own words, her public reason versus her secret reason, and 2-3 sub-personas.

**Language bank.** Problem words · desire words · emotion words · moment phrases · and the words to AVOID (supplier language, category jargon, anything the product cannot keep).

**Objection ranking.** Top 5 with strength, plus the first-use shock — what goes wrong or feels wrong the first time, which must be pre-answered or it becomes a refused delivery.

**Angles.** ALL viable ones with what each is built on. The user picks; we never pick for them.

**Compliance flags.** Claims to refuse or rewrite, raised here rather than after the page is built.

## Mapping the map onto the framework

| Framework slot | Map source |
|---|---|
| Q1 headline + sub-headline | Deep desire (6) + the moment (4) — never a feature |
| Pain point + desire turn | Problem in her words (2) + hidden habit (3) + painful moment (4) |
| The 5 reviews | Objection ranking + sub-personas (see review-frames.md) |
| Benefits deep dive (top 3) | Surface desire (5), each answered against a failed solution (9) |
| Us vs Others rows | Failed solutions (9) — categories only, never a named brand |
| Final CTA | Identity (7) + the real guarantee |
| FAQ (5-6) | Objections 1-6, phrased in her first person |
| Image prompts | Her world, her emotion, her words (Phase 7) |

## The rule

A line that cannot be traced to a map line does not go on the page. If a framework slot has nothing behind it, ask the user or mark it [HYPOTHESIS] and list it for validation — do not fill it with supplier adjectives.

## Validation (when the map is mostly [HYPOTHESIS])

Read reviews of the same product on AliExpress, Amazon and the local marketplaces — 5-star for the desire words, 3-star for the objections, 1-star for the fears. Collect the exact local phrases from TikTok comments and Facebook groups. Then ask every COD customer on the confirmation call: what made you order, what almost stopped you, what did you try before. After 20-30 answers, replace [HYPOTHESIS] with [VOC] and rewrite the sections that changed.
